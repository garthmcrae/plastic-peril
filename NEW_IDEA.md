You play against your child. You print the map. You defend an objective. The child plays against you.

This means the adult (game master) puts down a sheet, can read the story, tick off any hit points, select any monsters, and control the game. The child, armed with luck and a dice, enters the dungeon, cave, or forest and plays against you with a miniature of their choice. This means the game master can steer and ensure the game is as engaging, difficult, or easy as needed. Rules should be flexible and close to borderline cheating to keep it interesting.

## Setup

Before play begins, the child places the obstacles on the map. The printed sheet comes with an empty grid (entrance, exit, walls only) and a pile of obstacle cut-outs or tokens. The child decides where the boulders, pits, and asteroid-shaped rubble go — giving them ownership of the dungeon before they enter it. The adult can veto unplayable layouts (no path to exit) but otherwise lets the child compose the space.

## Playback

Setup. Dad unfolds the printed sheet on the kitchen table — a 10×10 dungeon, jaggy walls, an entrance up top, an exit at the bottom, a sidebar with three life boxes and a d6 encounter table. He picks a chipped plastic skeleton from the shelf and parks it on an obstacle tile two rows in. The kid grabs a troll doll with neon hair and plants it on the entrance tile.

> Dad: "You're in the Cave of the Lost Sock. Something's snoring down here. Roll."

**Turn 1.** Kid rolls a 4. Counts squares out loud — one, two, three, four — and lands on a tile with the skeleton. Encounter. She rolls again on the d6: a 1, Enemy. She rolls to kill: a 2. Wound. Dad crosses off a life box but pauses — she's been rolling badly all morning.

> Dad: "Actually the skeleton trips on its own foot. Roll again."

She rolls a 5. Skeleton dead. She narrates: the skeleton said sorry first and then fell over. Dad nods, removes the miniature.

**Turn 2.** Kid rolls a 6, moves the troll doll down the corridor. Lands on a treasure tile. Rolls a 4 on the d6 — treasure. Rolls again: a 5. Magic item. Dad reads from the sidebar: *Phoenix Feather — restore one life, any time*. She writes her name in wobbly letters next to it.

**Turn 3.** Dad places a Monster in My Pocket goblin on the path ahead — improvised, not on the map. "It heard you coming." Kid rolls a 3, walks straight into it. d6: a 5, Friend or Foe. Roll again: a 4. Friend. She decides the goblin is called Bin and he's lost his mum. Bin slots in next to the troll doll on the tile. +1 to her next kill.

**Turn 4.** Kid rolls a 2. Stops on a trap tile Dad had been saving. He looks at her face, looks at the one remaining life box, and quietly forgets about the trap.

> Dad: "Floor's clear. Keep going."

**Turn 5.** One row from the exit. Dad puts down the boss — a half-painted Space Marine missing an arm. Kid rolls a 5, charges in. Combat roll: 3, plus 1 from Bin, equals 4. Kill. The troll doll steps onto the exit tile. Dad circles WIN on the sidebar.

Total time: eleven minutes. Three rules bent. One goblin named. Sheet goes on the fridge.
