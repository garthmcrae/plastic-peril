# Dungeon!

Fantasy dungeon-crawling board game first released in 1975 by TSR. Players are adventurers exploring a multi-level dungeon in search of treasure while fighting monsters and escaping alive. The goal is to collect enough gold and return safely to the dungeon entrance.

## Components

- Multi-level dungeon board:
  - **Level 1** – weakest monsters and smallest treasures
  - **Level 2**
  - **Level 3**
  - **Level 4**
  - **Level 5** – strongest monsters and greatest rewards
- Monster cards
- Treasure cards
- Character cards with unique combat abilities
- Gold trackers or treasure totals
- Six-sided dice
- Player pawns

## Character Classes

Players choose fantasy adventurers inspired by classic roleplaying archetypes:

- Wizard
- Elf
- Hero
- Superhero

Different editions also include additional classes such as:

- Cleric
- Rogue
- Warrior

Each class has different combat strengths, treasure goals, and special abilities.

## Gameplay

2–8 players depending on edition. Players begin at the dungeon entrance and take turns moving through rooms and corridors.

### Turn Structure

On a turn:

1. Roll a die for movement.
2. Move through the dungeon.
3. Enter a room and reveal its monster.
4. Choose whether to fight or evade.

### Combat

Combat is simple and fast:

- Roll one die
- Compare the result to the monster's target number on the monster table
- Success defeats the monster and earns treasure
- Failure may force retreat or cause penalties

Different character classes have different odds against specific monsters.

## Monsters

Monsters become more dangerous in deeper dungeon levels.

Common monsters include:

- Giant Rats
- Skeletons
- Ogres
- Trolls
- Dragons
- Demons
- Vampires

Stronger monsters guard larger treasure rewards.

## Treasure

Defeating monsters awards treasure cards or gold values.

Treasure may include:

- Gold
- Magic items
- Rare artifacts
- Bonus rewards

Each character class has a different gold target needed to win.

## Winning the Game

To win, a player must:

1. Collect their required amount of treasure.
2. Escape the dungeon.
3. Return safely to the entrance space.

Greedy players can risk deeper dungeon levels for larger rewards, but stronger monsters make survival less certain.

## Special Features

- One of the earliest board games inspired directly by tabletop roleplaying games such as Dungeons & Dragons
- Fast, lightweight dungeon exploration compared to full RPG systems
- Push-your-luck gameplay encourages risky decisions
- Different character classes create asymmetric strategies
- Simple monster tables allow quick combat resolution without a game master

## Editions

### Original TSR Edition (1975)

Designed by David R. Megarry and published by TSR during the early rise of Dungeons & Dragons. Featured basic artwork and straightforward dungeon mechanics.

### 1981 Edition

Updated artwork and rules refinements while retaining the core gameplay structure.

### Wizards of the Coast Edition (2012)

A revised edition released by Wizards of the Coast featuring:

- Updated visual design
- Streamlined rules
- New character classes
- Faster setup and gameplay
- Revised monster balance
