# Overview

## Context

A physical dungeon-crawl game played on a printed A4 sheet with whatever miniatures you have — Warhammer figures, Monsters in My Pocket, D&D toys, 90s troll dolls. Anything on the table is fair game. Print and play; no special components required beyond a handful of standard dice.

Designed for two audiences that overlap more than expected: Warhammer hobbyists who want a quick narrative game, and kids who want to roll dice and kill things. Both are valid. Keep rules light enough that a kid can learn in one turn.

## References

- Hasbro Forbidden Tower (closest — printed sheet, dice-driven movement, encounter stops)
- HeroQuest (sidebar story, named rooms, encounter tables)
- Warhammer / AoS (miniatures language, wound tracking, stat-light combat)
- Monsters in My Pocket (creature variety from whatever's on the shelf)
- Dungeons & Dragons (trap/treasure/event vocabulary)
- Forbidden Bridge

## Proposed Gameplay

**Setup**
- Each player picks a miniature as their hero. Anything works — troll doll, Space Marine, owlbear.
- Place heroes at the Entrance tile. Place enemy/obstacle miniatures on obstacle tiles.
- Shuffle or agree on hero order; alternatively roll a die — highest goes first.

**Turn**
1. Roll a die. Move that many squares along open corridors.
2. If you enter a tile with an obstacle miniature, stop — you've hit an encounter.
3. Roll on the Encounter table (d6). Resolve immediately.
4. Pass your die to the next player.

**Encounter table (d6)**
| Roll | Result | Resolution |
|------|--------|------------|
| 1 | Enemy | Roll again — 4+ kills it, 1–3 you take a wound |
| 2 | Enemy | Same as above, tougher flavour |
| 3 | Trap | Take a wound automatically, then move on |
| 4 | Treasure | Take a token or note a bonus (extra move next turn) |
| 5 | Friend or Foe | Roll again — 4+ it's a friend, 1–3 it's a foe (resolve as Enemy) |
| 6 | Event | Read the map event |

**Friend or Foe**
Essential when playing with young children. On a 5, roll again to determine intent:
- **Foe** (1–3): resolve as an Enemy encounter
- **Friend** (4+): the miniature joins your party — place it next to your hero

A friend companion gives +1 to your next enemy kill roll (roll again and take the higher). They travel with you until you take a wound, at which point they flee (remove from board). Only one companion at a time.

**Magic Items**

One of each exists per game. When collected, write your name next to it on the sidebar. Cross it off when used — it's gone for the rest of the game.

Collected via Treasure (roll 4): roll again — 4+ you find a magic item (take the next uncollected one), 1–3 it's just gold (+1 move next turn).

| Item | Friendly name | Effect |
|------|--------------|--------|
| Revive | Phoenix Feather | Restore one crossed-off life box, any time |
| Instakill | Thunder Stone | Skip the combat roll — the enemy is dead |
| Undo | Rewind Rune | Take back any one roll or decision this game |

**Wounds / Lives**
- Heroes have 3 lives (marked on the sidebar).
- 0 lives = out. Last hero standing (or first to exit) wins.

**Winning**
- First hero to reach the Exit tile wins.

## Sidebar Format (per map sheet)

Each printed sheet carries a sidebar next to the map with:

1. **Title** — map name
2. **Story** — 2–3 sentences of flavour setting the scene
3. **Objective** — one sentence, e.g. "Reach the exit before your rivals"
4. **Hero Lives** — three printed boxes per player (2 players); cross them off with a pen as wounds are taken
5. **Encounter Table** — the d6 table printed on the sheet so nothing needs to be looked up
6. **Event** — one unique event specific to this map (drawn when a 5 is rolled)

## Miniatures & Components

- Any miniature works as a hero or enemy — scale and faction don't matter
- Recommended dice: 2–3 standard d6
- One pen per player — everything is written and crossed off directly on the printed sheet
- No board, no cards, no tokens required — the sheet is the only component besides dice and whatever's on the table

## Open Questions

- Should wound count vary by hero, or fixed at 3 for everyone? (currently 3, 2 players)
- Does treasure carry between maps (campaign mode) or reset each sheet?
- Is there a Scout mechanic — spend a move to roll and peek at the next tile's encounter before entering?
- Multiple players on the same tile — do they fight each other or cooperate?
- Are the references right, or is there a closer analogue we're missing?
- Can a companion be "upgraded" if you find a second friend — swap or keep both?
- Does the companion have a name line on the sidebar, or is that overkill at age 3?

## Ideas

- **Scout roll**: before moving into a tile, spend your action to roll — on 5+ you see what the encounter is and can choose to avoid it (backtrack one square)
- **Named enemies**: each obstacle tile could have a suggested miniature type printed lightly on the sheet (skeleton, orc, beast) — players use whatever they have that roughly fits
- **Campaign chain**: exit of one map is entrance of the next; heroes carry their wound state forward
- **Kid mode**: remove traps, double treasure results — faster and more rewarding for younger players
- **Tell the story**: after every encounter, the active player narrates what happened out loud before play continues. No wrong answers. For a 3-year-old the adult prompts: "what did the monster say?", "where did the treasure come from?", "is your new friend a good guy or a bad guy pretending?" This turns each roll into a shared story beat rather than just a result.
- **Name your companion**: when a friend is found, the child names them and decides their deal — are they a lost wizard, a baby dragon, a confused knight? Write the name on the sheet. They become a character, not just a bonus.
- **Monster voices**: when an enemy is placed, someone gives it a voice and a reason for being there before the combat roll. Keeps young players engaged with the fiction rather than just the dice.
