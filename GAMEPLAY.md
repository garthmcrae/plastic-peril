# Gameplay

You play against your child. You print the map. You are the game master. The child enters the dungeon armed with one d6, three life boxes, a miniature of their choice, and a fistful of luck.

The adult reads the story, ticks off life boxes, rolls the Enemy d6, and quietly steers the game. The child rolls everything else. Rules are flexible. Borderline cheating to keep it interesting is fine — it is the job.

## Setup

1. Print the sheet — the map already has obstacles on it.
2. Child picks toys/miniatures and places one on **every obstacle tile**. Those are the encounters. Bad guys, monsters, anyone who has wronged the household pet. Their choice.
3. Child puts their own mini on the entrance tile.
4. Adult reads the **narrative** opening paragraph out loud and notes the bracketed GM prompts.

## Turn loop

**Move.** Child rolls the d6 and moves up to that number of squares **or stops at an encounter** (the next obstacle tile with a toy on it), whichever comes first. Up, down, left, right.

**Fight (only if standing on or adjacent to a toy).**

- Child rolls the **Combat d6** on the sheet.
- Adult rolls the **Enemy d6** on the sheet.
- Both results happen in the same turn. Read them in order, narrate.
- Combat d6 slot 4 is always **"cast a spell"** → child rolls the **Spells d6** instead.
- Combat d6 slot 6 is always **a kill** → fight ends, remove the toy.
- If the Enemy d6 lands on a wound result, tick a life box.
- If neither side ended it, the fight continues next turn — move, then roll again.

**Win.** When the child reaches the exit tile, the adult reads the **ending** line on the sheet.

**Lose.** Three wounds and the child is out. The adult is allowed to forget the last wound if the child's face is doing The Thing.

## Example playback

Setup. Dad unfolds the sheet — a 10×10 cave, jaggy walls, entrance up top, exit at the bottom-left, sidebar with Life, Spells, Combat, Enemy, and a kind ending about a damp sock. Kid roots through the toy box. A chipped skeleton on the first obstacle. A pink Monster-In-My-Pocket goblin on the second. A faceless Lego knight on the third. Troll doll with neon hair on the entrance tile.

> Dad: "You're in the Cave of the Lost Sock. Something in the dark is humming. Roll."

**Turn 1.** Kid rolls a 4. Counts squares — one, two, three — and stops at the skeleton on tile three. Encounter.

- Combat d6: she rolls a 2. *You trip on your own boot, land on the enemy (wound).*
- Enemy d6: Dad rolls a 1. *Poos pants — no attack.*

She narrates the skeleton sitting down to deal with its problem. Dad nods, leaves the skeleton there for next turn.

**Turn 2.** Kid rolls a 5, but only moves one square to stay on the skeleton.

- Combat d6: a 4. *Cast a spell.* She rolls the Spells d6: a 3. *Stinkbreath.* Next enemy skips its roll.
- Enemy d6: Dad rolls a 6. *Shrugs it off and glares.* The skeleton is unbothered but is now downwind of stinkbreath.

**Turn 3.** Kid rolls a 6.

- Combat d6: a 6. *Critical kill.* Skeleton removed. She moves the remaining squares, lands one tile short of the goblin.
- (No enemy roll — fight was already over.)

**Turn 4.** Kid rolls a 2, walks into the goblin.

- Combat d6: a 3. *Solid whack — enemy takes a wound.* (Goblin still up.)
- Enemy d6: a 2. *Wild swing — you take a wound.* Dad ticks a life box.

**Turn 5.** Kid rolls again to stay and fight. Combat d6: 6. Goblin done. Dad looks at the remaining life boxes and the Lego knight up ahead. Quietly forgets that the next obstacle was supposed to be hard.

**Turn 6.** Kid rolls a 5, slips past the knight on a technicality (Dad: "he was looking the other way"), steps on the exit.

Dad reads the ending out loud. Damp sock. Kid laughs. Sheet goes on the fridge.

Total time: twelve minutes. Three rules bent. One stinkbreath.
